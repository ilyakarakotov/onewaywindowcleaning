Hard water stain removal for windows

Those white spots that won’t wash off are minerals stuck to the glass, usually from sprinklers or a leaky downspout. A normal window cleaning can’t touch them. Getting them off takes the right products, the right technique and some patience.

The cause

What causes hard water spots

Every time sprinkler water dries on a window, it leaves a thin layer of calcium and magnesium behind. One summer of the sprinkler hitting the same windows is enough to build a cloudy white haze. Leaky gutters, downspouts that splash onto glass and well water do the same thing.

Our approach

How we remove them

Hard water removal isn’t about scrubbing harder. Rough tools and the wrong chemicals can scratch glass or damage window coatings. We take a close look first, try a small area, and then work through the glass with a mineral remover and polish before a full clean.

  • A close look at the glass before any work starts
  • A test spot so you can see the result first
  • Mineral treatment and polishing, pane by pane
  • A full window cleaning to finish

Prevention

Keeping the spots from coming back

The best fix is usually the simplest one: turn the sprinkler heads so they don’t hit the windows, and fix any gutter or downspout that splashes onto glass. Regular window cleaning also stops new deposits from building up into stains that are hard to remove.

Questions

Common questions

Can every hard water stain be removed?

Most can. Fresh deposits usually come off completely. Stains that have sat on the glass for years can etch into it, and that damage can only be improved, not erased. We’ll tell you upfront what to expect.

Will it scratch my windows?

Not with the right products and a careful hand. That’s why we start with a test spot.

How long does it take?

It depends on how many windows are affected and how heavy the buildup is. We’ll give you a realistic idea with the free estimate.
