Hard water stain removal for windows

Those white spots that won’t wash off are minerals stuck to the glass, usually from sprinklers or a leaky downspout. A normal window cleaning can’t touch them. Getting them off takes the right products, the right technique and some patience.

The honest answer

Can these spots actually come off?

One Way Window Cleaning removes hard water stains from windows for homes in Everett, Snohomish, Seattle and Bellevue, WA. The white spots are mineral deposits left behind by sprinkler water and rain, and getting them off takes a different process than a normal window cleaning.

Most hard water spots can come off. Fresh mineral deposits usually lift completely, while stains that have sat on the glass for years may have etched into the surface, and etched glass can be improved but not made new again.

That is why we start with a close look and a test spot. You see what the result will look like on your worst window before you commit to the whole job, and we will tell you plainly if a pane is past what cleaning can fix.

The cause

What causes hard water spots

Every time sprinkler water dries on a window, it leaves a thin layer of calcium and magnesium behind. One summer of the sprinkler hitting the same windows is enough to build a cloudy white haze. Leaky gutters, downspouts that splash onto glass and well water do the same thing.

Around Puget Sound the pattern is easy to spot once you know it. The worst panes are usually the ground floor windows in line with a sprinkler head, and the glass under a dripping gutter corner.

Our approach

How we remove them

Hard water removal isn’t about scrubbing harder. Rough tools and the wrong chemicals can scratch glass or damage window coatings. We take a close look first, try a small area, and then work through the glass with a mineral remover and polish before a full clean.

  • A close look at the glass before any work starts
  • A test spot so you can see the result first
  • Mineral treatment and polishing, pane by pane
  • A full window cleaning to finish

If your glass is tinted or has a low-E or other coating, tell us when you book. Coated glass gets a gentler approach, and the test spot matters even more, because a coating can be damaged by methods that plain glass handles fine.

Limits

What hard water removal cannot fix

Cleaning cannot fix cloudiness that sits between the panes of a double pane window. That fog means the seal between the panes has failed and moisture is inside the unit itself. The surface can be spotless and the window will still look cloudy.

Etching is the other limit. When minerals sit long enough, they eat into the glass surface. Removal takes the deposits off and clears the window up a lot, but the etched pattern can still show in certain light. We would rather tell you that before the job than have you find it after.

DIY

Trying it yourself first

Vinegar is worth a try on light, fresh spots, and many homeowners start there. Soak a cloth, hold it on the spot for a few minutes, and wipe. If the spot fades, you have fresh deposits and patience may finish the job.

Where DIY usually stops working is bonded deposits. Once minerals have built up over a season or more, vinegar does little, stronger household cleaners can stain frames and sills, and abrasive pads or eraser style sponges can haze glass permanently. Our guide to removing hard water stains yourself, and where DIY stops working walks through each method and its limits honestly.

Prevention

Keeping the spots from coming back

The best fix is usually the simplest one: turn the sprinkler heads so they don’t hit the windows, and fix any gutter or downspout that splashes onto glass. Regular window cleaning also stops new deposits from building up into stains that are hard to remove.

If the splash is coming from above, gutter cleaning is part of the fix. An overflowing downspout will keep restaining the same windows every rain.

Cost

What affects the price

The price turns on how many panes are affected and how heavy the buildup is. A few sprinkler hit windows on the ground floor are a small job. A whole side of the house with years of staining, or stained glass on an upper floor, takes longer because every pane is treated and polished one at a time.

Questions

Common questions

Can every hard water stain be removed?

Most can. Fresh deposits usually come off completely. Stains that have sat on the glass for years can etch into it, and that damage can only be improved, not erased. We’ll tell you upfront what to expect.

Is this hard water, or is something wrong with the window?

If the cloudiness is on the surface and feels rough or chalky, it is usually mineral deposits. If the fog sits between the two panes of a double pane window and the surface feels smooth, that is a failed seal, and no cleaning can fix it. We will tell you which one you have before you pay for a removal that cannot help.

Will it scratch my windows?

Not with the right products and a careful hand. That’s why we start with a test spot. Tell us if your glass is tinted or has a low-E or other coating, because coated glass needs extra care and rough tools or the wrong chemicals can damage a coating even when plain glass would be fine.

Can I get the spots off myself with vinegar or CLR?

Vinegar can help on light, fresh spots, and it is worth trying on one small pane first. It does very little once minerals have bonded to the glass over a season or more. Household cleaners like CLR give mixed results and can stain frames and sills, and abrasive pads can haze the glass for good. If a careful test patch does not move the spots, stronger scrubbing is more likely to damage the window than clean it.

How do I stop the spots coming back?

Turn sprinkler heads so they do not hit the glass, and fix gutters and downspouts that splash onto windows. Then keep up regular window cleaning so new deposits get washed off before they build into stains.

How long does it take?

It depends on how many windows are affected and how heavy the buildup is. We’ll give you a realistic idea with the free estimate.

How much does hard water removal cost?

It depends on how many panes are affected, how heavy the buildup is, how long it has been sitting on the glass, and how easy the windows are to reach.
