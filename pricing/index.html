Window cleaning

What window cleaning typically costs

For a whole home, Angi’s 2026 cost data puts a typical window cleaning at about $220, with most jobs between $150 and $300 or more. Thumbtack’s Seattle figures land a little lower, with the average job at $165 to $180. An Everett pricing guide from Velocity Cleaning Systems breaks it down by home size: small homes up to 10 windows at $150 to $250, medium homes with 15 to 20 windows at $250 to $425, and large homes with 25 or more windows at $425 to $700 or more.

Companies quote this work three ways, and knowing which one you are looking at makes quotes easier to compare. Per window is the most common: HomeBlue puts Everett at $9 to $11 per standard window, and Angi’s national data runs $8 to $16 per window for inside and out, or $4 to $8 for one side only. Per pane shows up on homes with divided lights, at $5 to $7 per pane in HomeBlue’s Everett figures. The third way is one flat price for the whole job, which is what a careful estimate gives you once the home has been looked at.

What moves the price:

  • How many windows, and how big the glass is
  • Inside and out, or exterior only (exterior only often runs 40 to 60 percent less)
  • Stories and how hard the high glass is to reach
  • Buildup: a first clean after years of film takes longer than a maintenance clean
  • Extras some companies charge separately, like screens, tracks and skylights

Our window cleaning service includes frames, sills and screens in the cleaning. Your own price comes from the free estimate.

Hard water removal

What hard water removal typically costs

Hard water work is usually priced per affected window, on top of or instead of a standard cleaning. Angi lists hard water stain removal at $10 to $30 per window in its 2026 window cleaning cost data. Published pricing for this service is thinner than for the other three, so the factors below matter more than the range.

What moves the price:

  • How many windows have staining, and how much of each pane is covered
  • How long the deposits have sat: fresh spots lift faster than years of buildup
  • Whether the glass is etched, which can be improved but not always fully restored
  • Coated, tinted or low-E glass, which needs a gentler approach
  • Access, the same as any window work: height and reach set the time

Because etched glass cannot be made new again, the honest way to price this job starts with a look at the worst window. Our hard water removal service starts with a test spot, and the estimate tells you what result to expect before you pay for the whole job.

Pressure washing

What pressure washing typically costs

Pressure washing is usually priced by the square foot or as a flat price per surface. Angi’s Seattle cost page puts washing at $0.10 to $0.50 per square foot depending on the surface, with a driveway at $100 to $250 and a patio at $100 to $200 in the Seattle area. Small jobs often carry a minimum charge, because the trip and setup take the same time whether the surface is big or small.

What moves the price:

  • Square footage of the surface, first and most
  • The surface itself: concrete, pavers and steps each wash at a different pace
  • Moss, algae and oil buildup, which slow the work down
  • Access and water hookup distance
  • How many surfaces are done in the one visit

Our pressure washing service covers driveways, walkways, patios, steps and entryways. The estimate prices your surfaces exactly, before any washing starts.

Gutter cleaning

What gutter cleaning typically costs

Gutter cleaning is usually priced per linear foot of gutter. Angi’s Seattle data runs $1.26 to $1.56 per linear foot, which works out to $157 to $195 for 125 feet of gutter, $188 to $234 for 150 feet, and $251 to $311 for 200 feet. HomeBlue’s Seattle figures sit lower, at $0.70 to $1.20 per linear foot. Published per-foot numbers vary that much between sources, which is worth knowing when one quote looks far cheaper than another.

Height is the other big factor. Angi’s Seattle ranges by stories: one story $157 to $200, two stories $188 to $275, three stories $234 to $311. What moves the price:

  • Linear feet of gutter around the home
  • Stories and roof pitch, which set the ladder work
  • How full the gutters are, and whether downspouts are blocked
  • Tree cover: firs, cedars and maples fill gutters faster and deeper
  • Gutter guards, which have to be lifted or worked around

Our gutter cleaning service includes a downspout flow check and takes the debris away. Your price comes from the free estimate, set before the ladders come out.

Compare fairly

How to compare two quotes

Two quotes for the same house can be far apart and both be honest. They usually differ in what is included, not just in the number. Before you compare totals, line these up:

  • What is included in writing: glass only, or frames, sills and screens too
  • For gutters: whether downspouts are checked and debris is hauled away
  • Whether the price is exact, or an estimate that can change on the day
  • Whether the company is licensed, and who answers when you call
  • For hard water: whether anyone looked at the worst window before quoting

A very low quote often turns out to be a smaller job once the inclusions are read closely. A fair comparison is total price against the same list of work.

Common questions about cost

Are these the prices One Way Window Cleaning charges?

No. The ranges on this page are typical market ranges for the Seattle and Everett area, taken from published sources like Angi, Thumbtack and HomeBlue and checked in October 2026. Your price from One Way comes from a free estimate, and you will know the exact price before any work starts.

How much does window cleaning cost around Everett and Seattle?

Most homes in this area land between $150 and $300 or more for a full window cleaning, based on published market data. Thumbtack puts the average Seattle job at $165 to $180, and Everett pricing guides quote $9 to $11 per standard window. Inside and out costs more than exterior only, and large homes with a lot of glass run higher.

Why do quotes for the same house vary so much?

Companies price in different units, per window, per pane, per square foot, per linear foot or one flat price for the job, and they include different things. One quote may cover screens, frames and sills while another is glass only. Comparing the total price and the written list of what is included is more reliable than comparing the unit price.

Each service is priced on its own factors, so there is no set bundle price to quote here. One visit can cover windows, gutters and the driveway in a single trip. Mention everything you want done when you ask for the estimate, and each part will be priced out in the same quote.
