How often to clean gutters, windows and driveways in the Pacific Northwest

National advice says clean everything once a year and call it done. That schedule was not written for a place with nine months of wet weather and fir trees that drop needles in every season. Here is the rhythm that actually fits homes around Everett, Snohomish, Seattle and Bellevue.

Gutters first

Gutters: at least twice a year

If you only remember one schedule from this page, make it the gutters. A clogged gutter does its damage quietly all winter, soaking the fascia board behind it and dumping water next to the foundation every time it rains. Regular cleaning is how you keep that damage from building up.

The two anchor cleanings are late fall and spring. Late fall, after the maples and other broadleaf trees have dropped, clears the system before the heaviest rain of the year arrives. Spring clears what winter added: needles shaken loose by storms, seed pods, and the grit that washes off the roof. A home in the open, with no big trees over the roof, can often run on exactly those two.

Most Puget Sound homes are not in the open. Douglas firs, western red cedars and pines shed needles all year, with a heavier drop in summer stress and winter storms, and needles are the worst gutter debris there is. They mat together, slip through or under gutter guards, and pack downspouts at the elbows. If a conifer hangs over your roof, plan on three or four cleanings a year, or at least a mid-winter look during a heavy rain: if water is spilling over the edge, the schedule no longer matters, they need cleaning now.

Gutter guards change the mix, not the need. Guards keep big leaves out, but grit, needles and seeds still get through and mat on top of the guard itself, so guarded gutters still need occasional attention, just less often. Windows

Windows: spring after pollen, then again before the gray months

Two cleanings a year suit most homes here. The spring one matters most, and timing it after the spring pollen settles saves you paying to wash a yellow film back onto the glass a week later. The second cleaning lands in late summer or fall, so the glass is clear going into the months when daylight is scarce and dirty windows make rooms feel darker than they are.

Some homes run faster than that schedule. Houses near Puget Sound or Lake Washington pick up a salt and moisture film from marine air. Homes on busy roads collect road grime. And any house under trees gets sap mist, needle dust and the green tint that grows on glass that stays damp. If your windows look tired again two months after a cleaning, your house is one of those, and it may need a third cleaning each year.

Regular cleaning also keeps the cheap problems cheap. Sprinkler spots and mineral film that come off easily in their first season can bond to the glass over years. The window cleaning page explains what a visit covers, inside and out or exterior only.

Concrete

Driveways and walkways: about once a year

Concrete, pavers and steps in this climate grow a film of moss and algae anywhere they stay damp, and damp here means most of the year. North-facing surfaces and anything under tree canopy go green first. Once a year is the working rhythm for most homes, and spring is the best slot: the wet season has done its worst, and the surface gets to dry and stay clean through summer.

Treat slippery steps and walkways as a safety item, not a cosmetic one. A green film on shaded steps is genuinely treacherous in the rain, and if a surface has reached that point it does not need to wait for its annual slot. Washing removes the growth and the slick layer with it. It does not stop regrowth forever, because shade and drainage decide how fast it returns, which is why the yearly rhythm exists. The year

The local year, season by season

  • Spring: windows after pollen settles, gutters cleared of winter debris, driveway and walkways washed after the wet season.
  • Summer: check sprinkler aim so glass is not being restained, and glance at gutters under conifers during any heavy rain.
  • Late summer to fall: second window cleaning before the gray months, and watch for the first leaf drop.
  • Late fall: the big gutter cleaning, after leaves and needles are down and before the heaviest winter rain.
  • Winter: mostly watching. Overflow in a storm is the signal to act, not the calendar.

Honest split

DIY or hire it out?

Plenty of this is realistic DIY. Ground-floor windows, a one-story gutter line you can reach from a well-set ladder, and a rented washer on a flat driveway are all fair weekend work.

The line most homeowners draw, and we think it is the right one, is the second story. Second and third story glass and gutters mean hours spent at the top of a ladder, often on wet ground, reaching sideways. That is where the injuries in this trade come from, and it is the main reason people hire the high half of the house out while still doing the easy panes themselves. Time is the other honest factor: a full house of windows inside and out can take a homeowner most of a day, and gutters on a two-story home are a project, not a chore.

Do the split that suits your house. There is no rule that says one company has to do every pane.

Questions

Common questions

How often should you clean gutters in Washington?

At least twice a year: once in late fall after the leaves and needles are down, and again in spring. Homes under Douglas firs, cedars or big maples often need three or four cleanings a year, because conifers drop needles in every season, not just fall. If water spills over the edge during rain, the gutters are already overdue.

What is the best time of year to clean gutters?

Late fall is the most important cleaning, after leaf drop and before the heaviest winter rain arrives, so the system starts the wet season empty. Spring is the second one, clearing the winter needle fall and seed debris before the dry months. Under heavy conifer cover, add a mid-winter or mid-summer check.

How often should windows be cleaned in the Seattle and Everett area?

Twice a year suits most homes: once in spring after the pollen settles, and again in late summer or fall before the gray months. Homes near the water, on busy roads or under a lot of trees usually benefit from an extra cleaning, because salt film, road grime and tree debris build up faster there.

How often should you pressure wash a driveway?

About once a year in this climate, ideally in spring after the wet season, so the surface starts the dry months clean. Shaded, north-facing and under-tree concrete grows moss and algae faster and may need a second wash, because the slippery film is a safety problem on steps and walkways, not just a looks problem.

Can I just do all of this myself?

Ground-floor windows and a one-story gutter line are realistic DIY for many people. The honest split most homeowners land on is doing the easy work themselves and hiring out the second-story glass and gutters, where the job means hours on a ladder. Time counts too: a full house of windows can take a homeowner most of a day.
